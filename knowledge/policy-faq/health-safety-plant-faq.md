# Policy FAQ — Health, Safety, Environment and Plant Visits

FirstGear reports operating under ISO 45001 (occupational health and safety), ISO 14001 (environment) and ISO 50001 (energy), with HIRA, lock-out/tag-out (LOTO), regular audits and mandatory DOJO safety training, and a zero lost-time injury frequency rate in FY 2025-26. The procedures, timelines and rules below are an illustrative model for the Compass training programme — follow the plant's posted EHS rules where they differ.

Key rules at a glance: complete the safety induction before entering any shop floor. Wear the PPE issued at the gate (safety shoes, safety glasses, ear protection in marked areas, high-visibility vest). Walk only in marked pedestrian walkways. Never touch running machines or enter LOTO areas. No photography without written permission. Report every incident or near miss within 24 hours. Anyone may stop unsafe work.

### Q: Do I need safety training before visiting a plant?
Yes. Every employee completes the online EHS induction in the first week and the plant-specific safety briefing at the gate before the first visit to each plant. Shop-floor access is blocked on your ID card until the induction is recorded. Source: EHS FAQ §1 Induction.

### Q: What PPE must I wear on the shop floor?
Safety shoes, safety glasses and a high-visibility vest at all times; ear protection in noise zones marked with the blue sign; gloves only where the area sign requires them (never near rotating machines). Visitor PPE is issued at the gate; return it when leaving. Source: EHS FAQ §2 PPE.

### Q: Can I walk anywhere in the plant?
No. Use the marked green pedestrian walkways, keep clear of forklift and crane routes, and stay out of restricted areas (heat treatment, forging presses, electrical rooms, chemical stores) unless escorted by the area supervisor. Source: EHS FAQ §3 Movement.

### Q: What is lock-out/tag-out (LOTO)?
A procedure that isolates and locks energy sources before anyone works on a machine. Never touch, operate or reach into a machine, and never remove a lock or tag — even during a customer walk-through or a quick check of a part. Source: EHS FAQ §4 LOTO.

### Q: What does "anyone may stop unsafe work" mean?
If you see a situation that could hurt someone — a missing guard, an unsafe lift, a customer visitor wandering near a running press — you may and should ask for the work to stop and inform the area supervisor. No one is penalised for stopping work for safety. Source: EHS FAQ §5 Stop work.

### Q: How do I report an incident or near miss?
Tell the area supervisor immediately, get first aid if needed, and record it in the EHS reporting app or with the plant EHS officer within 24 hours. Near misses (nothing happened, but could have) are reported too — they are how accidents are prevented. Source: EHS FAQ §6 Reporting.

### Q: What should I do in an emergency at a plant?
Follow the alarm and the evacuation signs to the assembly point, do not use lifts, help visitors you are escorting, and stay until the head count is complete. Emergency numbers are posted at each gate and on your ID card. Source: EHS FAQ §7 Emergencies.

### Q: What are the rules for bringing a customer to the plant?
Plant Head approval, registration at least 48 hours ahead, an NDA where confidential processes will be seen, the gate safety briefing and PPE for every visitor, and an escort at all times. Agree the route with the plant in advance and keep the group within the walkways. Source: EHS FAQ §8 Customer visits.

### Q: Can visitors take photographs in the plant?
No, unless the Plant Head has given written permission for specific areas, for example for an approved audit. Phones may be used for calls only in designated areas. This protects safety and confidential processes. Source: EHS FAQ §9 Photography.

### Q: Are there rules about alcohol and drugs?
Yes. Reporting to work, driving on company business or entering a plant under the influence of alcohol or drugs is prohibited and is a serious disciplinary matter. Prescribed medication that affects alertness must be reported to the plant medical officer. Source: EHS FAQ §10 Fitness for work.

### Q: What are the safe driving rules on company business?
No hand-held phone use while driving, seat belts for all, helmets on two-wheelers, speed limits always, no driving more than 4 hours without a break or more than 300 km a day, and avoid self-driving after 22:00. Report any road incident to EHS. Source: EHS FAQ §11 Driving.

### Q: Is first aid available?
Every plant has a first-aid room and trained first-aiders on each shift; headquarters has first-aid kits on each floor and trained first-aiders listed near them. Major plants have an occupational health centre with a medical officer. Source: EHS FAQ §12 First aid.

### Q: What environmental rules apply to me?
Segregate waste in the marked bins, never dispose of chemicals, oils or batteries in normal waste, switch off equipment and lights, and report leaks or spills to EHS. The company tracks energy, water and emission intensity and has reported reductions in all three in FY 2025-26. Source: EHS FAQ §13 Environment.

### Q: Why do customers ask about our EHS performance?
OEMs include safety, environmental and sustainability criteria in supplier assessments and questionnaires. Use published facts — ISO 45001, ISO 14001 and ISO 50001, zero LTIFR in FY 2025-26, energy, emission and water-intensity reductions, captive solar projects — and route detailed questions to EHS. Source: EHS FAQ §14 Customer questionnaires.

### Q: Can I carry samples or parts in my car?
Small samples with a delivery challan, properly packed and secured, yes. Heavy, sharp or hazardous items (oils, batteries, chemicals) must go through logistics. Never keep parts loose in the passenger area. Source: EHS FAQ §15 Samples.

### Q: What should I wear for a customer plant visit?
Business attire with your own safety shoes if you visit customer plants often (the company reimburses one pair a year for field roles), and follow the customer's PPE rules, which may be stricter than ours. Source: EHS FAQ §16 Customer sites.

### Q: Do customer site rules apply to me?
Yes. At a customer's plant you follow their safety rules and instructions in addition to ours. Report any incident at a customer site to the customer's host and to FirstGear EHS within 24 hours. Source: EHS FAQ §16 Customer sites.

### Q: What is the policy on working at height or in confined spaces?
Office staff, including KAMs, do not work at height or enter confined spaces. If an inspection requires it, a trained and permitted plant person does it while you observe from a safe area. Source: EHS FAQ §17 High-risk work.

### Q: How do I raise a safety concern at headquarters?
Raise a facilities ticket or tell the Admin or EHS team; urgent hazards (wet floor, exposed wiring, blocked exits) should be reported immediately by phone. Source: EHS FAQ §18 Office safety.

### Q: Are there health check-ups?
Employees above 35 years are offered an annual preventive health check-up through the insurer's network; plant staff have statutory medical check-ups. The employee assistance programme supports mental wellbeing. Source: EHS FAQ §19 Health.

### Q: What is a safety observation tour?
A short walk where managers, including Key Accounts staff when at a plant, observe work and talk to operators about safe behaviour. New KAMs join one during the plant walk in their onboarding. Source: EHS FAQ §20 Safety tours.

### Q: What is a near miss and why report it?
An event that could have caused injury or damage but did not — for example a part falling from a rack near someone. Reporting near misses lets the plant fix causes before someone gets hurt; it is encouraged and never penalised. Source: EHS FAQ §6 Near misses.

### Q: Are pregnant employees given special safety arrangements?
Yes. Inform HR and your Reporting Boss confidentially; plant visits to noise or chemical areas are avoided, and travel and working arrangements are adjusted. Source: EHS FAQ §21 Special needs.

### Q: Is safety part of performance reviews?
Yes. Following EHS rules is a condition of employment, and safety behaviour is considered in appraisals for everyone who visits plants. Serious violations are disciplinary matters. Source: EHS FAQ §22 Accountability.

### Q: What should I do if a customer asks to see a process that is restricted?
Explain that access is restricted for safety or confidentiality and offer an alternative: a supervised view from a safe area, a process flow description by the plant engineer, or approved documentation. Escalate to the Plant Head if the customer insists. Source: EHS FAQ §8 Restricted processes.

### Q: Who investigates incidents?
The plant EHS team with the area manager, using root-cause analysis; serious incidents are reviewed by the Plant Head and reported to senior leadership. Corrective actions are tracked to closure. Source: EHS FAQ §23 Investigation.

### Q: Who do I contact about health, safety and plant visits?
The plant EHS officer at each site, or the Head of EHS. Source: EHS FAQ §24 Contacts.
