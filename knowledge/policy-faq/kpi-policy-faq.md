# Policy FAQ — KAM KPI and Performance Policy

Model policy for the FirstGear Compass training programme. Weights, targets and payout factors here are illustrative model policies for training — your own scorecard is agreed with your Reporting Boss each April. The KPIs themselves are the ones in the KAM Charter.

Key rules at a glance — the KAM scorecard (weights total 100%):
- Revenue versus plan 25%
- Contribution margin and price realisation 15%
- Collections and DSO 15%
- On-time-in-full delivery (OTIF) 10%
- Customer PPM (quality) 10%
- RFQ hit rate 10%
- New business and EV programme wins 10%
- Customer satisfaction 5%
Ratings 1–5 from the weighted achievement; variable-pay factor: rating 5 → 150%, 4 → 120%, 3 → 100%, 2 → 50%, 1 → nil.

### Q: What KPIs is a KAM measured on?
The KAM Charter KPIs: revenue versus plan, on-time-in-full delivery (OTIF), customer PPM, RFQ hit rate, receivables (DSO) and customer satisfaction — plus contribution margin and new business wins in the annual scorecard. Each has a weight, a target agreed in April, and a defined data source. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §1 Scorecard.

### Q: What are the KPI weights?
Revenue versus plan 25%, contribution margin and price realisation 15%, collections and DSO 15%, OTIF 10%, customer PPM 10%, RFQ hit rate 10%, new business and EV programme wins 10%, customer satisfaction 5%. Weights can be adjusted by up to 5 points per KPI for a specific account with the Head of Key Accounts' approval, keeping the total at 100%. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §1 Weights.

### Q: How is revenue versus plan measured?
Net invoiced sales to your accounts (after credit notes) from the ERP, compared with the annual operating plan (AOP) phased by month. Achievement = actual ÷ plan. Tooling invoices are reported separately and do not count towards piece-part revenue. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §2 Revenue.

### Q: What if revenue falls because the customer's volumes drop?
The KPI still reflects actual revenue, but your Reporting Boss considers documented market-driven volume changes (for example a customer programme ending early) in the qualitative assessment. Flag volume risks early in the monthly review — late surprises are judged more harshly than early warnings. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §2 Revenue.

### Q: How is contribution margin measured?
The account's contribution margin (net revenue minus variable costs, from Finance's account profitability report) against the plan, and price realisation — whether annual price-downs, index adjustments and engineering-change prices were implemented as approved. Any quotation below the 15% margin floor without CFO approval scores zero on this KPI for the year. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §3 Margin.

### Q: How is DSO measured?
Days sales outstanding = average receivables ÷ revenue × days in period, from Finance's credit-control report, compared with the standard 60-day payment terms. The target is DSO of 65 days or less and no invoice overdue by more than 90 days without an agreed plan. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §4 Collections.

### Q: Why is the KAM responsible for collections?
Because the KAM owns the customer relationship. Finance runs credit control, but resolving disputes that hold payments (pricing, short shipments, quality debits) needs the KAM. Review the overdue list every week and agree a plan for every disputed invoice. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §4 Collections.

### Q: How is OTIF measured?
On-time-in-full = schedule lines delivered on time and in full ÷ total schedule lines, from SCM's delivery report against the customer's call-offs. Target 95% or higher (or the customer's own target if stricter). SCM and the Plant own delivery performance; the KAM is measured on it because they coordinate recovery and communication. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §5 Delivery.

### Q: How is customer PPM measured?
Defective parts per million = parts rejected by the customer ÷ parts shipped × 1,000,000, from the Quality system, for the rolling 12 months. The target is the customer's PPM target or 50 PPM, whichever is stricter. Quality owns containment and 8D; the KAM is measured on timely communication and on 8D closures within the deadline. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §6 Quality.

### Q: How is RFQ hit rate measured?
RFQs won ÷ RFQs quoted, by lifetime value, from the CRM, over the year. Target 30% or as agreed for the account. RFQs declined at feasibility (documented no-go) are excluded, so honest feasibility decisions do not hurt you. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §7 RFQ hit rate.

### Q: What counts as a new business win?
A nomination or letter of intent received in the year for a new part, platform or programme, valued by lifetime revenue, with extra weight for EV programmes in line with the company's electrification priority. Carry-over business on running parts does not count. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §8 New business.

### Q: How is customer satisfaction measured?
From the customer's supplier scorecard where available, plus an annual satisfaction survey run by the Key Accounts office, on a 1–5 scale. The target is 4 or higher. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §9 Satisfaction.

### Q: How is my overall rating calculated?
Each KPI's achievement (capped at 120%) is multiplied by its weight and summed to give a composite score. Composite of 110% or more → rating 5 (outstanding); 100–109% → 4 (exceeds); 90–99% → 3 (meets); 75–89% → 2 (partly meets); below 75% → 1 (below expectations). Your Reporting Boss may adjust by one rating level for documented behaviours (integrity, teamwork, customer feedback), reviewed by the Head of Key Accounts. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §10 Rating.

### Q: How does the rating affect variable pay?
The variable-pay factor applies to your target variable pay: rating 5 → 150%, 4 → 120%, 3 → 100%, 2 → 50%, 1 → nil. A proven Code of Conduct breach (for example bribery, fraud or leaking customer prices) reduces variable pay to nil for that year regardless of KPIs. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §11 Variable pay.

### Q: When are KPIs reviewed?
Monthly at the weekly internal account review's month-end session (data refresh), quarterly with your Reporting Boss in a formal scorecard review, mid-year in October, and finally in the April appraisal. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §12 Reviews.

### Q: Where can I see my KPI data?
In the Key Accounts dashboard (refreshed monthly from ERP, Quality, SCM, CRM and Finance). If a number looks wrong, raise it with the data owner — Finance for revenue, margin and DSO; SCM for OTIF; Quality for PPM; the CRM administrator for RFQs — before the quarterly review. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §13 Data.

### Q: When do KPIs start for a new KAM?
During the first 30 days you are measured on onboarding readiness (tasks, assessments, scenarios and gates), not on account KPIs. Account KPIs start from the month after your Day-30 READY sign-off, with targets pro-rated for the rest of the year. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §14 New KAMs.

### Q: What happens to my KPIs if I take over an account mid-year?
Targets are pro-rated from the handover month. Issues inherited from the previous KAM (for example an overdue invoice or open 8D) are documented in the handover note so they are assessed fairly. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §14 Handover.

### Q: Are KPIs the same for every account?
The KPI set is the same, but targets differ by account (customer PPM targets, AOP revenue, DSO terms). Strategic EV accounts may have a higher weight on new business; mature ICE accounts may weigh margin and collections more. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §1 Weights.

### Q: What is the difference between KPIs and onboarding readiness?
Onboarding readiness (tasks, Day-15 four-pillar score, Day-21 scenario test, Day-30 sign-off) decides whether you are ready for independent account handling. KPIs measure business results once you handle the account. Both are visible to your Reporting Boss. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §14 New KAMs.

### Q: Can I appeal my rating?
Yes. Raise a written appeal with your HRBP within 15 days of the appraisal discussion. The Head of Key Accounts and HR review the data and the reasons; the decision is final. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §15 Appeals.

### Q: What behaviours are assessed beside KPIs?
The company values — integrity, vitality, frugality and agility — as shown in account work: never committing without approval, early warning to customers, cost discipline and fast responses. Customer and colleague feedback are used. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §10 Behaviours.

### Q: How should a KAM prepare for the quarterly scorecard review?
Bring the account dashboard, explanations for any KPI below target with a recovery plan, the RFQ pipeline, the overdue-receivables plan, open 8Ds and the customer's latest supplier scorecard. Keep it factual — the review is a coaching conversation. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §12 Reviews.

### Q: Is there an incentive for EV programme wins?
EV programme nominations carry extra weight inside the new-business KPI (lifetime value counts at 1.25×), reflecting the company's electrification priority. There is no separate per-deal commission. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §8 New business.

### Q: Do sales commissions exist?
No. KAMs are not paid commission per deal; variable pay is based on the balanced scorecard so that revenue is never pursued at the expense of margin, quality, delivery or compliance. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §11 Variable pay.

### Q: How do quality escalations affect my KPIs?
Customer PPM affects the quality KPI. Missing an 8D deadline without informing the customer, or a customer escalation caused by late communication, is also considered in the behavioural assessment. Fast containment and honest early updates are recognised. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §6 Quality.

### Q: What is an account plan and how does it link to KPIs?
The annual account plan sets the account's AOP revenue, margin, RFQ pipeline, new programmes, risks and the governance calendar. KPI targets come from it. Submit it to your Reporting Boss in April and update it quarterly. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §16 Account plan.

### Q: What is the governance calendar for KPI tracking?
Weekly internal account review (quality, delivery, commercial, programme topics); monthly customer operations review; quarterly business review (QBR) with customer leadership, prepared with the Reporting Boss. KPI data feeds each of them. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §12 Governance calendar.

### Q: How is OTIF counted when the customer changes a schedule late?
Schedule changes inside the agreed frozen window are measured against the original schedule only if accepted by SCM in writing; otherwise the line is excluded and documented. Record such changes in the CRM and the delivery-review minutes. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §5 Delivery.

### Q: What if a customer pays late because of its own internal issue?
DSO still counts it, but agreed payment plans and escalations made on time are considered in the assessment. Escalate overdue invoices through the agreed customer contact and Finance after 15 days overdue. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §4 Collections.

### Q: Does the company publish team or account rankings?
No public rankings. The Head of Key Accounts reviews all accounts quarterly to share good practice and allocate support. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §17 Transparency.

### Q: How do targets get set each year?
In March–April, Finance shares the AOP by account; the KAM proposes targets with an account plan; the Reporting Boss and Head of Key Accounts agree them, and they are entered in the HR portal goal sheet by 30 April. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §18 Target setting.

### Q: What if my KPI targets change during the year?
Targets change only for significant events (account transfer, programme cancellation, acquisition) with the Head of Key Accounts' approval, recorded in the goal sheet with the effective date. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §18 Target changes.

### Q: How are KPIs handled when I am on long leave?
For approved leave of more than 60 days (for example maternity), targets are pro-rated for the time worked and the covering KAM's period is assessed separately. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §19 Long leave.

### Q: Does the AI assistant calculate my KPIs?
No. Ask FirstGear explains how KPIs are defined and shows your onboarding progress; account KPIs come only from the Key Accounts dashboard and are reviewed by people. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §13 Data.

### Q: What is the minimum KPI performance for confirmation?
For confirmation after probation the Reporting Boss uses the Day-30 sign-off and early account performance and behaviour; there is no fixed KPI threshold in the first six months. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §14 New KAMs.

### Q: Who decides if a KPI data dispute is valid?
The data owner corrects the data if it is wrong; if the dispute is about interpretation, the Head of Key Accounts decides, consulting Finance. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §13 Data.

### Q: Are mentors measured on their KAMs' readiness?
Yes. Senior KAMs who mentor have a goal in their scorecard for the quality and timeliness of their reviews and the readiness of their mentees, assessed by the Head of Key Accounts with HR. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §20 Mentors.

### Q: What KPI-related reports should a KAM send each month?
A one-page account update before the monthly review: revenue and margin versus plan, OTIF and PPM, overdue receivables, RFQs submitted and won, open 8Ds and risks, with actions and owners. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §12 Monthly update.

### Q: Who do I contact about the KPI Policy?
Your Reporting Boss (Head of Key Accounts) for targets and reviews; your HRBP for appraisal and variable-pay questions. Source: KPI Policy FAQ §21 Contacts.
