# Policy FAQ — Compliance and Ethics

Model policy for the FirstGear Compass training programme, consistent with the Code of Conduct. FirstGear reports mandatory annual e-learning with an assessment on the Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual harassment, insider trading, competition and anti-trust law, and health and safety, and training on anti-bribery, anti-money laundering and trade sanctions. Thresholds and procedures below are illustrative model policies for training — the published Code of Conduct and Legal's guidance prevail.

Key rules at a glance: gifts or hospitality above a nominal ₹5,000 must be declared to Compliance and are normally declined; cash or cash equivalents are never acceptable. No payment or benefit to win or keep business. No discussion of prices, margins, capacity or bids with competitors. Declare conflicts of interest annually and when they arise. Designated persons follow the insider-trading code. Report concerns through the ethics line without fear of retaliation.

### Q: What is the Code of Conduct?
The company's rules for honest and lawful business: gifts and hospitality, anti-bribery, competition law, confidentiality, conflicts of interest, fair treatment, safety and speaking up. Every employee signs it at joining and completes the e-module every year. It is guided by the value of integrity — always do the right thing, no matter what the cost or whether anyone is looking. Source: Compliance FAQ §1 Code of Conduct.

### Q: Can I accept a gift from a customer?
Small token gifts of nominal value (company-branded items, festival sweets) can be accepted. Gifts above ₹5,000 must be declared to Compliance and are normally declined politely. Cash, gift cards, vouchers or anything offered during a negotiation or tender are never acceptable. Source: Compliance FAQ §2 Gifts.

### Q: How do I declare a gift?
Use the gift and hospitality register in the employee portal within 5 working days, with the giver, occasion and estimated value. Compliance decides whether you keep, return or donate it. Source: Compliance FAQ §2 Gift register.

### Q: Can I accept a customer's invitation to dinner or an event?
Occasional, modest business meals with a clear business purpose are fine. Lavish hospitality, travel or leisure events paid by a customer or supplier need prior approval from Compliance. Never accept hospitality while you are evaluating that party's request or during a negotiation. Source: Compliance FAQ §3 Hospitality.

### Q: What is bribery?
Offering, giving, requesting or accepting anything of value to improperly influence a business decision — money, gifts, favours, jobs for relatives, travel, discounts or donations. It is illegal and banned whether done directly or through an agent or intermediary, and regardless of local practice. Source: Compliance FAQ §4 Anti-bribery.

### Q: What is a facilitation payment and is it allowed?
A small unofficial payment to a government official to speed up a routine action (for example clearing goods or issuing a permit). It is not allowed. If someone demands one, refuse, inform Legal, and — if your safety is threatened — protect yourself first and report afterwards. Source: Compliance FAQ §4 Facilitation payments.

### Q: Who counts as a public official?
Government employees, employees of public-sector undertakings (including government-owned customers such as railway authorities), customs and tax officials, regulators and political office-holders. Any gift, hospitality or payment to them needs prior written approval from Legal and Compliance. Source: Compliance FAQ §5 Public officials.

### Q: Can we use agents or consultants to win business?
Only with Legal's due diligence, a written contract with anti-bribery clauses, a fee that matches the services, and the CFO's approval. Payments must be made to a bank account in the agent's name in their country of business. Source: Compliance FAQ §6 Third parties.

### Q: What is a conflict of interest?
Any situation where your personal interest could influence, or appear to influence, your work decisions — for example a relative working at a customer, an investment in a supplier, or a side business. Conflicts are not always wrong, but they must be declared so they can be managed. Source: Compliance FAQ §7 Conflicts of interest.

### Q: How do I declare a conflict of interest?
In the annual conflict-of-interest declaration in the employee portal, and immediately when a new conflict arises. Your Reporting Boss and Compliance agree how to manage it (for example moving the account to another KAM). Source: Compliance FAQ §7 Declarations.

### Q: What does competition law mean for a KAM?
Never agree or exchange information with competitors on prices, margins, capacity, bids, customers or markets — formally or informally, including at industry associations and supplier days. Do not discuss these with a competitor's employees even socially. If such a discussion starts, leave, make your objection clear and report it to Legal. Source: Compliance FAQ §8 Competition law.

### Q: Can I share our prices with a customer who asks what competitors charge?
Share only our own approved prices with that customer. Never ask a customer to pass you a competitor's quotation, and do not use competitor price information obtained improperly. Market information from public sources or the customer's general feedback is fine. Source: Compliance FAQ §8 Competitor information.

### Q: What is insider trading and why does it matter to me?
FirstGear is listed on the NSE and BSE. Unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI) — for example large orders, results, acquisitions or the loss of a major customer — must not be used to trade in FirstGear shares or a customer's shares, or shared with anyone who might trade. Breaches are serious offences under SEBI's insider-trading regulations. Source: Compliance FAQ §9 Insider trading.

### Q: Am I a designated person under the insider-trading code?
The Company Secretary designates employees with access to UPSI, which often includes senior KAMs handling large orders. Designated persons must disclose their holdings, get pre-clearance for trades above the threshold in the code, and must not trade when the trading window is closed (typically from the end of each quarter until 48 hours after results are published). You will be informed in writing if you are designated. Source: Compliance FAQ §9 Designated persons.

### Q: Can I talk about a big order we just won?
Not until the company has published it. Large orders can be price-sensitive; Corporate Communications and the Company Secretary decide on disclosure. Keep the information within the people who need it. Source: Compliance FAQ §9 UPSI.

### Q: How do I raise an ethics concern?
Speak to your manager or HR, or use the confidential ethics line (whistle-blower / vigil mechanism) by phone, web or email; anonymous reports are accepted. Reports are reviewed independently, and serious cases can be raised with the Chair of the Audit Committee. Source: Compliance FAQ §10 Speaking up.

### Q: Will I be protected if I report a concern?
Yes. Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith is prohibited and is itself a disciplinary offence. Knowingly false reports are not protected. Source: Compliance FAQ §10 Non-retaliation.

### Q: What happens after I report a concern?
Compliance acknowledges it within 7 days, assesses it and, where needed, appoints an investigator independent of the people involved. You may be asked for more information. Outcomes are shared with you to the extent confidentiality allows. Source: Compliance FAQ §10 Investigations.

### Q: Are political donations allowed?
No. FirstGear does not make political contributions through employees, and you must not use company funds or time for political activity. Personal political activity is your own, outside work. Source: Compliance FAQ §11 Political activity.

### Q: Can I give to a customer's charity event?
Charitable giving is done only through the company's CSR programme, approved by the CSR committee — never as a personal or account-level donation linked to a customer, which could look like a bribe. Source: Compliance FAQ §12 Donations.

### Q: What are trade sanctions and export controls?
Laws that restrict business with certain countries, entities and people, and the export of certain technologies. Legal screens new customers, distributors and destinations. Check with Legal before quoting to a new overseas customer or shipping to a new country. Source: Compliance FAQ §13 Sanctions.

### Q: What is anti-money laundering about for a KAM?
Be alert to unusual payment requests: payments from third parties on a customer's behalf, cash, payments to or from unrelated countries, or requests to split invoices. Report them to Finance and Legal before accepting. Source: Compliance FAQ §14 Anti-money laundering.

### Q: How should I treat confidential information from a previous employer?
Do not bring, use or share it — drawings, prices, customer lists or plans from a previous employer must not enter FirstGear systems. If someone offers you a competitor's confidential information, refuse and inform Legal. Source: Compliance FAQ §15 Confidential information.

### Q: Can I accept a job offer for a relative from a customer or supplier?
Recommending relatives to a customer or supplier is a conflict of interest that must be declared. FirstGear does not offer jobs or internships to customer employees' relatives as a favour; recruitment is merit-based through HR. Source: Compliance FAQ §7 Relatives.

### Q: What records must be accurate?
All business records: quotations, approvals, invoices, expense claims, delivery and quality documents, and CRM entries. Falsifying, backdating or hiding records is a serious breach. Source: Compliance FAQ §16 Accurate records.

### Q: What is the company's approach to human rights in the supply chain?
FirstGear prohibits child and forced labour and supports freedom of association and safe workplaces, in its own operations and expects the same of suppliers. Report concerns through the ethics line. Source: Compliance FAQ §17 Human rights.

### Q: How do data-protection rules apply?
Personal data of employees and customer contacts must be collected for a clear purpose, kept secure, used only for that purpose and deleted when no longer needed, in line with India's data-protection law and customer requirements. See the IT and Information Security FAQ. Source: Compliance FAQ §18 Data protection.

### Q: What compliance training must I complete?
The annual e-modules with assessments — Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual harassment, prohibition of insider trading, competition and anti-trust law, and health and safety — plus anti-bribery, anti-money-laundering and trade-sanctions training where assigned. New joiners complete them within 30 days. Source: Compliance FAQ §19 Training.

### Q: What happens if someone breaches the Code of Conduct?
It is investigated and can lead to disciplinary action up to termination, recovery of losses, and reporting to authorities where the law requires. Variable pay for the year is forfeited for proven serious breaches. Source: Compliance FAQ §20 Consequences.

### Q: Does the AI assistant replace Compliance advice?
No. Ask FirstGear answers from approved documents to help you understand the rules; for any specific situation — especially gifts, public officials, competitors or UPSI — ask Legal and Compliance before acting. Source: Compliance FAQ §21 Getting advice.

### Q: Can I accept a supplier's gift if I do not deal with that supplier?
The same rules apply to all business partners, whether or not you deal with them directly: nominal token gifts are fine, anything above ₹5,000 is declared and normally declined, and cash or vouchers are never acceptable. If unsure, declare it in the gift register and let Compliance decide. Source: Compliance FAQ §2 Gifts.

### Q: What should I do if a customer employee asks for a personal favour?
Politely decline anything personal — jobs for relatives, travel, discounts on our products, help with personal purchases — and inform your Reporting Boss and Compliance. Favours to customer employees can be bribery even when nothing is asked in return. Source: Compliance FAQ §4 Personal favours.

### Q: Can I invest in a customer's or supplier's shares?
Holdings in listed customers or suppliers are allowed only if they are not material, are declared in the conflict-of-interest declaration, and you never trade on unpublished information about them gained through your work. Designated persons follow the insider-trading code for FirstGear shares. Source: Compliance FAQ §7 Investments.

### Q: Who do I contact about compliance and ethics?
Legal and Compliance for advice; the Company Secretary for insider-trading questions; the ethics line for concerns. Source: Compliance FAQ §22 Contacts.
