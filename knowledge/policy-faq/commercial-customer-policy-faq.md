# Policy FAQ — Commercial and Customer Policies

Model policy for the FirstGear Compass training programme, consistent with the Commercial Approval Authority Matrix and the Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide. Limits and timelines here are illustrative model policies for training — the Approval Authority Matrix and Finance's current instructions prevail.

Key rules at a glance: standard payment terms 60 days; any change needs CFO approval. Credit limits are set by Finance. Only authorised signatories sign contracts; KAMs never sign commitments. A mutual NDA must be in place before exchanging drawings. Penalties, liquidated damages and unlimited liability are never accepted without Legal and CFO approval. Standard warranty is as per contract, normally 24 months from dispatch or the customer's standard where agreed by Legal.

### Q: What are FirstGear's standard payment terms?
60 days from the invoice date. Any change to payment terms needs CFO approval regardless of value, because longer terms tie up working capital; Finance costs each additional 15 days before you negotiate. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §1 Payment terms.

### Q: How are customer credit limits set?
Finance sets a credit limit for each customer from its financial strength, payment history and expected business, and reviews it annually or when business changes significantly. The KAM provides the business forecast. Orders beyond the limit are blocked until Finance approves. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §2 Credit limits.

### Q: Who can sign a customer contract?
Only authorised signatories under the board-approved delegation of authority — typically the Head of Key Accounts with the CFO, or the Managing Director for strategic agreements. A KAM prepares and negotiates within approved positions but never signs contracts, LOIs, warranty agreements or letters of comfort. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §3 Signing authority.

### Q: When do I need an NDA?
Before exchanging drawings, specifications, cost data or any confidential information with a customer, prospect or partner. Use Legal's standard mutual NDA; customer NDA formats go to Legal for review. Record the NDA in the CRM with its expiry date. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §4 NDAs.

### Q: Can I accept the customer's general terms and conditions?
Not without Legal's review. Customer terms often include unlimited liability, penalties, broad warranty, IP ownership or set-off rights. Legal marks up the deviations; the Head of Key Accounts and CFO approve the negotiation position. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §5 Terms and conditions.

### Q: What clauses always need Legal and CFO approval?
Penalties and liquidated damages, uncapped or consequential-damage liability, warranty longer than the standard, most-favoured-customer pricing, exclusivity, IP transfer, payment terms beyond 60 days, consignment stock, and price-freeze commitments. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §5 Critical clauses.

### Q: What is our standard warranty?
As set out in the contract — normally 24 months from dispatch, or the customer's vehicle warranty-linked terms where Legal has agreed them. Warranty extensions and recall-cost sharing are negotiated only with Legal, Quality and CFO approval. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §6 Warranty.

### Q: Can I promise a customer a price freeze?
No. Price commitments beyond the quotation validity (90 days) or contract terms need approval under the Approval Authority Matrix, and price freezes always need CFO approval because raw-material indices move. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §7 Pricing commitments.

### Q: Can I offer a discount to win an RFQ?
Only an approved price. Any reduction from the approved quotation is a new pricing proposal: the Head of Key Accounts approves at or above the margin floor; the CFO below the floor; the Managing Director for strategic below-floor pricing. Never indicate a discount verbally before approval is recorded. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §7 Discounts.

### Q: How long is a quotation valid?
90 days unless otherwise approved, and it must state the raw-material index basis. After expiry, re-confirm inputs and approval before re-submitting. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §7 Quotation validity.

### Q: What is a consignment or warehouse agreement?
An arrangement where our stock is held at or near the customer and invoiced on consumption. It affects working capital, insurance and stock risk, so it needs Finance, SCM and Legal review and CFO approval before you agree to it. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §8 Consignment.

### Q: Can I agree to hold safety stock for a customer?
Only with SCM's capacity confirmation and Finance's approval of the inventory cost, documented in a safety-stock agreement that states quantities, review frequency and who pays for obsolete stock. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §8 Safety stock.

### Q: Who owns the intellectual property in our designs?
FirstGear owns its designs, processes and tooling know-how unless a contract approved by Legal says otherwise. Customer-funded development may give the customer certain rights; never agree IP terms without Legal. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §9 Intellectual property.

### Q: What information can I share with a customer?
Information needed for the business relationship and approved for sharing: quotations, technical documents for their parts, delivery and quality data for their parts, and the approved corporate presentation. Never share internal cost models, overhead rates, margins, other customers' information, or unpublished financial data. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §10 Information sharing.

### Q: Can I share another customer's information as a reference?
No. Customer names, volumes, prices and programmes are confidential. Published facts from the corporate presentation (for example the number of EV programmes) can be shared; anything customer-specific needs that customer's written consent. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §10 Confidentiality.

### Q: How are price changes communicated to the customer?
In writing, with the approved new price, the reason (index adjustment, engineering change, annual agreement), supporting data and effective date. Keep the customer's acceptance in the CRM; Finance updates the price list only after approval and acceptance. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §11 Price changes.

### Q: What if the customer refuses an index adjustment?
Escalate to the Head of Key Accounts with the contract clause and index data. Do not stop supplies or threaten the customer; agree a meeting with commercial leadership. Unrecovered adjustments are reported in the monthly review. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §11 Disputes.

### Q: Can I stop supplies because a customer is not paying?
No. Only Finance (credit hold) with the Head of Key Accounts informed, and the CFO for strategic customers, can stop supplies. Stopping a customer's line has major consequences and is a last resort after escalation. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §12 Credit hold.

### Q: How should I handle a customer request for a letter of comfort or guarantee?
Send it to Finance and Legal. Guarantees, comfort letters, bank guarantees and performance bonds create financial liability and need CFO approval and an authorised signatory. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §13 Guarantees.

### Q: What are Incoterms and who decides them?
Incoterms define where delivery, risk and cost transfer from us to the customer (for example FCA, DAP, EXW). The standard is agreed in the quotation with SCM logistics and Finance; changes affect cost and risk, so they need Finance approval. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §14 Incoterms.

### Q: Can we invoice in foreign currency?
Yes, for export customers as agreed in the contract (usually USD or EUR). Finance manages currency risk and may require exchange-rate clauses for long programmes. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §14 Currency.

### Q: How do we treat tooling in quotations and invoices?
Tooling is quoted separately from piece price, paid as a lump sum at PPAP approval or amortised over agreed volumes. Amortisation beyond three years, tooling waivers and customer-funded tooling exceptions need CFO approval. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §15 Tooling.

### Q: What is our policy on customer audits of our books?
Customer cost audits or open-book reviews are allowed only where the contract provides for them, with Finance leading and Legal agreeing the scope. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §16 Cost audits.

### Q: What do I do if a customer asks for a volume-based rebate?
Treat it as a pricing proposal: Finance models the cost, the approval follows the Approval Authority Matrix, and the rebate terms (volume bands, payment timing) are written into the agreement reviewed by Legal. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §17 Rebates.

### Q: Can I discuss pricing with a competitor at a customer's supplier day?
Never. Do not discuss prices, margins, capacity or bidding intentions with competitors anywhere. Leave any such conversation and report it to Legal. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §18 Competition law.

### Q: What is the policy on dual sourcing requests?
If a customer plans to dual-source our part, inform the Head of Key Accounts immediately, understand the reason (price, capacity, risk), and prepare a response plan with Finance, SCM and Quality. Do not lower prices without approval. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §19 Dual sourcing.

### Q: How are customer claims for cost recovery handled?
Every claim (premium freight, sorting, line stop, warranty) needs documented evidence and a clear cause. Quality and SCM determine responsibility; Finance books agreed claims; settlements above ₹5 lakh need Head of Key Accounts approval and above ₹25 lakh the CFO. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §20 Claims.

### Q: Can we claim from a customer for volume shortfall?
Yes, where the contract provides for it (for example tooling under-recovery when volumes fall short of the amortisation basis). Prepare the claim with Finance and the contract clause, and present it through the Head of Key Accounts. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §20 Volume shortfall.

### Q: How are e-auctions or online bids handled?
Participation needs prior approval of a floor price by the Head of Key Accounts (or CFO below the margin floor). Bid only within the approved limit; record the approval and the bid history in the RFQ file. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §21 E-auctions.

### Q: Can I accept a customer's request to backdate a document?
No. Never backdate quotations, POs, certificates or delivery documents. Doing so is a Code of Conduct breach. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §22 Integrity.

### Q: What is the policy on customer-specific documentation fees or portal fees?
Fees charged by customers or their portal providers are reviewed by Finance and approved by the Head of Key Accounts before acceptance, and included in the account profitability. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §23 Customer fees.

### Q: How are customers with financial difficulties handled?
If you notice payment delays, insolvency news or unusual requests, inform Finance immediately. Finance may reduce credit limits, require advance payment or security, and the Head of Key Accounts decides the commercial approach. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §24 Credit risk.

### Q: Do these commercial policies apply during onboarding?
Yes — and a KAM in onboarding has no pricing or commitment authority at all. From Day 22, guided pricing exposure means you prepare positions that are decided jointly with the Reporting Boss, after Gate 2 and Reporting Boss approval. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §25 Onboarding.

### Q: Who do I contact about the commercial and customer policies?
Finance (cost controller, credit control) for pricing, credit and claims; Legal and Compliance for contracts and NDAs; the Head of Key Accounts for approvals. Source: Commercial Policy FAQ §26 Contacts.
