# FirstGear Company Overview

About this profile: "FirstGear" is the training alias of a real automotive and mobility-technology company listed on the NSE and BSE. Its name and identifying names (founders, directors, joint-venture partners, acquired companies and strategic partners) are withheld for training. Every figure below is as publicly reported by the company in its Annual Report 2025-26 (financial year April 2025 – March 2026) and its Q1 FY2026-27 results (April–June 2026). INR figures are in million (mn) or billion (bn) as reported; 1 crore = 10 mn.

## 1 Who we are
FirstGear is a mobility-technology company that designs, manufactures and supplies highly engineered, mission-critical systems and components for global OEMs in electric, personalised, intelligent and connected mobility. It is a leading supplier of driveline solutions for the global auto market and of traction-motor solutions for the Indian electric-vehicle (EV) market, and it has expanded into railways (brake systems, couplers and suspension systems). Capabilities span precision forging, mechanical and electrical systems, radar sensors and software development.

- Headquarters: Gurugram, Haryana, India.
- Listed on the Indian stock exchanges (NSE and BSE) since 2021.
- 12 manufacturing and assembly facilities across India, the USA, Mexico and China.
- 5 R&D centres.
- Total workforce: 6,691 at the end of FY 2025-26 (5,019 a year earlier); women 6.5% of the workforce.
- 49% of sales are to international markets.

## 2 History and milestones
- 1995 — Incorporated as a precision-forgings joint venture between an Indian promoter group and a Japanese materials company; pioneered precision-formed bevel gears in the Indian automotive market.
- 1998 — Began producing differential bevel gears at its first plant in Gurugram.
- 1999 — Opened a plant in Chennai, Tamil Nadu.
- 2005 — Opened a plant in Pune, Maharashtra.
- 2008 — Acquired the precision-forging business of a German steel group (the inventor of the precision-forged bevel-gear technology).
- 2013 — Opened an assembly plant in Michigan, USA; 100 million differential gears produced.
- 2016 — Opened an assembly plant in China.
- 2017 — Units II and III in Gurugram started; assembly plant in Mexico opened; 1 million differential assemblies produced.
- 2019 — Acquired an Indian starter-motor and electric-motor maker (adding motor capability); a global private-equity investor became an investor; opened the Manesar differential-assembly plant.
- 2020 — 250 million gears produced; first BLDC traction-motor contracts.
- 2021 — Listed on the Indian stock exchanges.
- 2022 — 100,000 traction motors produced.
- 2023 — Acquired a majority stake in a Serbia-based mmWave radar-sensing company; second Chakan (Pune) plant launched; 5 million differential assemblies produced.
- 2024 — New plant in Silao, Mexico.
- 2025 — Completed the acquisition of the railway equipment division of a listed Indian engineering group (consideration INR 16,426 mn), entering the railway sector.
- April 2026 — 500 million differential gears and 10 million differential assemblies produced.

## 3 Mission, vision and values
- Mission: Be leaders in the technology we invest in and give our customers an edge over the rest while designing and building products that will enable the future of mobility.
- Vision: To become one of the world's most respected and valuable mobility-technology companies for our customers, employees and shareholders.
- Values: Integrity — always do the right thing, no matter what the cost or whether anyone is looking. Vitality — build better products. Frugality — build better products more economically. Agility — build better and more economical products faster.

## 4 How the values show up in key account work
- Integrity means never committing a price, date or quality promise that has not been approved and recorded, and telling the customer bad news early with a plan.
- Vitality means bringing the customer better solutions — for example proposing an EV differential assembly instead of loose gears.
- Frugality means protecting cost and margin: challenge cost assumptions before conceding price.
- Agility means fast, reliable responses — for example the company redesigned motor architectures within four weeks during the 2025 rare-earth magnet export controls so that customer schedules were not affected.

## 5 Strategy — four priorities
1. Electrification — grow EV content per vehicle (EV differential assemblies, EV transmission gears, traction motors, motor controllers, suspension motors). Environmental target: 45% of revenue from EV-focused products.
2. Global market significance — global market share of 8.7% in differential gears and 4.2% in starter motors (calendar year 2025).
3. Diversification — across geographies ("Look East": India and China together 56% of FY 2025-26 revenue), products (the top four products made 86% of revenue in FY 2024-25; in FY 2025-26 that share was spread across the top eight), and segments (non-automotive revenue rose from 9% to 31%, helped by railways and off-highway).
4. Technology — new product verticals in sensors (in-cabin and short-range radar), controllers, railways, robotics (robots, cobots, humanoids, AGVs and AMRs) and eVTOL/drone powertrains.

Return on capital employed 15.4%, return on equity 13.2%. The company is net cash (INR 10,262 mn at 31 March 2026, after paying for the railway acquisition). Management attributed the margin pressure to higher commodity and labour costs, a mix shift towards lower-margin traction motors, and a lag in passing costs through to customers.

## 7 Electrification and order book
- BEV revenue FY 2025-26: INR 11,542 mn — 35% of automotive product revenue (36% in FY 2024-25). Q4 FY 2025-26 was the best-ever quarter for BEV revenue and BEV share.
- EV programmes: 67 programmes across 35 customers at 31 March 2026 (9 programmes and 3 customers added in the year). By June 2026: 69 programmes across 36 customers.
- Net order book: INR 237 bn (about USD 2.5 bn) at 31 March 2026, around 70% from EV programmes; INR 57 bn of new orders were won in FY 2025-26. By June 2026 the net order book was about INR 240 bn (about 5.4 times FY 2025-26 revenue).
- Net order book means aggregate revenue from awarded programmes that have yet to start or fully ramp up over the next 10 years, net of programmes reaching end of life, with a discount for possible launch delays. It includes the railway order book (purchase orders largely executed within 12 months).
- Notable FY 2025-26 wins: INR 15.5 bn for differential assemblies with final-drive gear on a new electric passenger-vehicle platform of a traditional North American OEM (largest single order in 2.5 years); INR 8.2 bn for integrated motor controller modules for active suspension from an Asian EV OEM and a European luxury performance OEM; INR 2.6 bn for EV three-wheeler drive motors from an Indian OEM; the first order for the Mexico driveline plant (a North American recreational off-highway vehicle OEM); and a programme for a hybrid platform.

## 8 Revenue mix
- Geography (FY 2025-26): India became the largest end market at 51% of revenue (29% in FY 2024-25). Q1 FY 2026-27: India 50%, Europe 26%, North America 15%, rest of Asia 9%.
- Segments: non-automotive revenue (railways, off-highway and others) 31% in FY 2025-26, up from 9%.
- Customer concentration: the year showed the risk of dependence on an anchor EV customer — despite that customer's weakness and a 16% fall in US BEV industry sales, BEV revenue fell only 5.6%, because of diversification across customers, programmes, products and geographies.

## 9 Industry context (as cited by the company)
- Global light-vehicle sales were about 91.94 million units in calendar 2025 (+3.7%), the first year above the 2019 level of 89.9 million. S&P Global Mobility expects a 1.6% decline in 2026.
- Electric cars were about 21 million units in 2025 (+20%), roughly one in four cars sold globally; the IEA expects 23 million (28% share) in 2026.
- India: domestic passenger-vehicle sales were an all-time high of 4.64 million units in FY 2025-26 (+7.9%); commercial vehicles 1.08 million (+12.6%); tractor volumes crossed one million for the first time.
- Railways: the Union Budget 2026-27 allocated a record INR 2,930 bn capital expenditure for Indian Railways (+10.5%).

## 10 Key risks the company reports
Geopolitical and tariff uncertainty; supply-chain disruption and material availability (rare-earth magnets, electronic components, special steels); raw-material price volatility (cost pass-through lags); pandemic-type disruptions; regulatory change; and the transition from ICE to electric vehicles, which affects ICE-dependent product lines.

## 11 Sustainability, safety and governance
- Certified management systems: ISO 14001:2015 (environment), ISO 50001:2018 (energy, all manufacturing sites) and ISO 45001 (occupational health and safety).
- FY 2025-26: zero lost-time injury frequency rate (LTIFR); emission intensity down 15–17%, energy intensity down 13–14% and water intensity down 25% year on year; rooftop solar generated 16,847 GJ (about 4% of energy use); captive solar contracted in Haryana (14.85 MWp) and Maharashtra (4.00 MWp), and a 2.5 MWp group-captive facility under way at Chennai; a 70,000 litres-per-day zero-liquid-discharge plant.
- ESG ratings: CRISIL ESG score 62 ("Strong"), ranked 2nd of 56 listed auto-component companies assessed.
- CSR spend INR 121 mn in FY 2025-26; a CII Industrial Innovation Award (Top 100 innovating companies).
- Mandatory e-learning with assessment on the Code of Conduct, prevention of sexual harassment, insider trading, competition/anti-trust law and health and safety; anti-bribery, anti-money-laundering and trade-sanctions training for employees and directors.
