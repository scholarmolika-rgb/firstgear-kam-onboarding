# Customer Escalation Playbook

## 1 Escalation levels
- Level 1 — KAM and the responsible function lead; response within 24 hours.
- Level 2 — Head of Key Accounts and function heads; response within 48 hours.
- Level 3 — Managing Director and Plant Head; for line-stop risk, safety issues or strategic relationship risk.

Escalate early: a risk shared early with a recovery plan protects trust; a surprise destroys it.

## 2 Delivery-risk escalation
When a delivery risk appears: confirm the facts with SCM and the Plant (quantity at risk, dates, cause); agree a recovery plan with options (overtime, alternate line, partial shipments, premium freight); inform the customer early with the recovery plan and a confirmed next update time. Never promise a date that SCM and the Plant have not confirmed in writing. Premium freight requires approval by the Head of SCM, and the cost owner must be agreed before it is used.

## 3 Quality escalation
Containment first: protect the customer's line. Involve Quality immediately, follow the complaint timelines in the Quality Governance Manual, and keep the customer informed with the 8D status. Do not speculate on root cause before D4.

## 4 Commercial escalation and price challenges
When a customer challenges a price: listen and capture the ask in writing; do not concede in the meeting; analyse with Finance using cost breakdown and index data; prepare a position with give-gets; obtain approval at the right level of the Approval Authority Matrix; respond by an agreed date.

## 5 Internal escalation
If a function is not meeting a customer commitment, raise it first with the function lead, then at the weekly internal account review, then to the Head of Key Accounts. Record the escalation and the agreed action.
