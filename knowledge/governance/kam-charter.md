# KAM Charter

## 1 Purpose
The Key Account Manager is the single accountable owner of the customer relationship. The KAM protects customer trust, revenue and margin by orchestrating the company's functions on the customer's behalf.

## 2 Account ownership model
The KAM owns: the relationship and communication with the customer, commercial negotiation within approved authority, the account plan, and coordination of cross-functional actions. Functions own execution: Engineering owns technical solutions, Quality owns containment and 8D, SCM and the Plant own delivery performance, Finance owns cost models and credit. The KAM does not do the functions' work — the KAM makes sure it happens, on time, and is communicated correctly.

## 3 Governance calendar
- Weekly internal account review (KAM-led): quality, delivery, commercial and programme topics.
- Monthly customer operations review with the customer's purchasing and quality teams.
- Quarterly business review (QBR) with customer leadership, prepared with the Reporting Boss.

## 4 KPIs
Revenue versus plan; on-time-in-full (OTIF) delivery; customer PPM (defective parts per million); RFQ hit rate; receivables (DSO); customer satisfaction score.

## 5 Escalation map
Escalations follow the Escalation Playbook: Level 1 KAM and function lead (24 hours), Level 2 Head of Key Accounts and function heads (48 hours), Level 3 Managing Director and Plant Head.

## 6 Onboarding principle
Learn first, then earn access. A new or reassigned KAM gains customer and pricing exposure through readiness gates and Reporting Boss approval — never automatically with time.
