# Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide

Knowledge guide for KAMs. It explains how prices are built and changed. It does not grant any pricing authority.

## 1 Cost build-up
A piece price is built from: material cost (typically 55–65% of the price), conversion cost (machine and labour time from the Plant's cycle time), overheads, logistics and packaging, SG&A, and margin. Every element must trace to a documented input from the RFQ file.

## 2 Tooling and development cost
Tooling and development cost is recovered either as a one-time lump-sum payment by the customer or amortised into the piece price over an agreed volume. Amortisation exposes the company to volume risk: if volumes fall short, tooling is under-recovered. Amortisation beyond three years needs CFO approval.

## 3 Margin logic
Finance sets a target contribution margin of 22% and a margin floor of 15%. Below the floor, a quotation needs CFO approval. Margin is protected by challenging cost assumptions first, not by cutting margin.

## 4 Price-change mechanics
- Raw-material index adjustments: steel, aluminium and copper indices are reviewed quarterly with a three-month lag, both up and down.
- Annual productivity price-downs (long-term agreements) are typically 1–3% and are negotiated against productivity plans.
- Engineering-change price impacts are quoted through the RFQ process.
- Commercial claims (for example premium freight or volume shortfall) require documented evidence.

## 5 Payment-term mechanics
Standard terms are 60 days. Longer terms increase working capital; each additional 15 days is costed by Finance before negotiation. Any change needs CFO approval.

## 6 Negotiation preparation
Before any negotiation the KAM prepares a brief: objective, walk-away position, the customer's likely position, give-gets, evidence (index data, cost breakdown), and the approvals already obtained. A KAM never concedes beyond an approved position in the room; new asks are taken back for approval.
