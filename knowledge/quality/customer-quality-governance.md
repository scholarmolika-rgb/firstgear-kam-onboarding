# Customer Quality Governance Manual

## 1 Customer-specific requirements
Each OEM issues customer-specific requirements (CSRs) on top of IATF 16949. CSRs define PPAP expectations, complaint response timelines, labelling and escalation rules. The KAM must know the CSRs of their customers and involve Quality before committing to any quality-related date.

## 2 APQP overview
Advanced Product Quality Planning runs in five phases: (1) plan and define the programme; (2) product design and development; (3) process design and development; (4) product and process validation; (5) feedback, assessment and corrective action. KAM touchpoints: confirming customer requirements in phase 1, timing and change communication through phases 2–4, and launch feedback in phase 5.

## 3 PPAP overview
The Production Part Approval Process demonstrates that the production process can consistently make parts that meet requirements. Submission levels run from 1 to 5; Level 3 (full submission with samples) is the default unless the customer specifies otherwise. Parts may not be shipped for production before PPAP approval or a documented customer deviation.

## 4 Complaint handling
On a customer complaint: acknowledge within 24 hours; start containment within 24 hours (sort stock at the plant, in transit and at the customer); open an 8D. The interim containment report (D3) is shared within 24–48 hours; root cause (D4) within 7 days; the full 8D with permanent corrective action within 30 days unless the CSR is stricter. Quality owns the 8D content; the KAM owns customer communication and makes sure dates are met.

## 5 8D discipline
D1 team; D2 problem description; D3 interim containment; D4 root cause; D5 permanent corrective action; D6 implementation and validation; D7 prevention of recurrence; D8 closure and recognition.

## 6 Engineering change control
No change to product, process, material, supplier or location may be made without the customer's approval, which normally requires a PPAP resubmission. Changes flow ECR (request) → impact assessment → ECN (notice) → customer approval → implementation. The KAM communicates change requests and their commercial impact; Engineering and Quality own the technical assessment.
