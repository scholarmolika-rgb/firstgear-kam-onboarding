# KAM FAQ — Quality, Programmes, Delivery and Escalation

The governing documents are the Customer Quality Governance Manual, Programme Governance — Nomination to SOP, the Customer Escalation Playbook and the Manufacturing Process Overview.

### Q: A customer reports a defect. What are the first steps?
Acknowledge within 24 hours, start containment within 24 hours (sort stock at the plant, in transit and at the customer) and open an 8D. Involve Quality immediately. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §4.

### Q: What are the 8D timelines?
Interim containment report (D3) within 24–48 hours, root cause (D4) within 7 days, and the full 8D with permanent corrective action within 30 days unless the customer's CSR is stricter. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §4.

### Q: Who owns the 8D?
Quality owns the 8D content. The KAM owns customer communication and makes sure the dates are met. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §4 and KAM Charter §2.

### Q: What are the eight disciplines?
D1 team; D2 problem description; D3 interim containment; D4 root cause; D5 permanent corrective action; D6 implementation and validation; D7 prevention of recurrence; D8 closure and recognition. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §5.

### Q: The customer asks me for the root cause on day one. What do I say?
Do not speculate before D4. Confirm the containment status and give the date when root cause will be shared. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §3.

### Q: The customer threatens a line stop. What do I do?
Containment first — protect the customer's line. Escalate to Level 3 (Managing Director and Plant Head) for line-stop risk, and keep the customer informed on a confirmed update cadence. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §1 and §3.

### Q: What are CSRs?
Customer-specific requirements issued by each OEM on top of IATF 16949. They define PPAP expectations, complaint timelines, labelling and escalation rules. Know your customers' CSRs and involve Quality before committing to any quality-related date. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §1.

### Q: What is APQP and where does the KAM fit in?
Advanced Product Quality Planning in five phases: plan and define; product design and development; process design and development; product and process validation; feedback, assessment and corrective action. The KAM confirms customer requirements in phase 1, communicates timing and changes in phases 2–4 and brings launch feedback in phase 5. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §2.

### Q: What is PPAP and which level is the default?
The Production Part Approval Process shows that production can consistently make parts that meet requirements. Levels run 1–5; Level 3 (full submission with samples) is the default unless the customer specifies otherwise. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §3.

### Q: Can we ship production parts before PPAP approval?
No — only with PPAP approval or a documented customer deviation. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §3 and Programme Governance §2.

### Q: Can we change a process, material or supplier to save cost?
Not without the customer's approval, which normally requires PPAP resubmission. Changes flow ECR → impact assessment → ECN → customer approval → implementation. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §6.

### Q: What are the programme milestones from nomination to SOP?
Nomination → kick-off → design freeze → tooling kick-off → off-tool samples → PPAP approval → SOP → launch stabilisation (first 90 days). Source: Programme Governance §1.

### Q: What must I check at nomination?
That the commercial basis — piece price, tooling, volumes and index basis — is recorded exactly as approved. Source: Programme Governance §2.

### Q: Who leads APQP and launches?
NPD leads programme launch and APQP from nomination to SOP. Quality owns PPAP submissions. The KAM tracks dates and communicates with the customer. Source: Organisational Navigation §1.

### Q: SCM says a delivery is at risk. What do I do?
Confirm the facts with SCM and the Plant (quantity at risk, dates, cause); agree a recovery plan with options (overtime, alternate line, partial shipments, premium freight); tell the customer early with the plan and the time of the next update. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §2.

### Q: Can I promise the customer a recovery date?
Only a date that SCM and the Plant have confirmed in writing. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §2 and Approval Authority Matrix §5.

### Q: Who approves premium freight?
The Head of SCM. Agree the cost owner before premium freight is used. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §2 and Organisational Navigation §1.

### Q: What are the escalation levels?
Level 1 — KAM and function lead, response within 24 hours. Level 2 — Head of Key Accounts and function heads, within 48 hours. Level 3 — Managing Director and Plant Head, for line-stop risk, safety issues or strategic relationship risk. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §1.

### Q: A function is not meeting a customer commitment. How do I escalate internally?
First the function lead, then the weekly internal account review, then the Head of Key Accounts. Record the escalation and the agreed action. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §5.

### Q: How does traceability help in a quality complaint?
Each part or batch carries a lot code linked to the material heat lot, machine, shift and test results, so Quality can contain a suspect lot precisely instead of sorting all stock. Source: Manufacturing Process Overview §4.

### Q: Which manufacturing operations are critical for our products?
Precision forging, heat treatment (hardness and case depth), finish machining of special characteristics and end-of-line testing (gear noise for EV differentials, electrical performance for motors). Source: Manufacturing Process Overview §3.

### Q: How do customers order parts?
Through delivery schedules; plants ship against Advance Shipping Notices (ASN), with Kanban for high-runner parts. Export customers are often served through the overseas assembly plants. Source: Manufacturing Process Overview §5.

### Q: Which plant makes what?
Gurugram, Manesar and Pune/Chakan make differential gears and assemblies; Chennai makes starter motors and traction motors and has an SMT line for radar sensors; the US, Mexico and China sites do final assembly and finishing. Source: Manufacturing Process Overview §1.

### Q: Where does a supply risk like rare-earth magnets show up?
In traction motors, which depend on magnets. In 2025 the company redesigned motor architectures within four weeks to protect customer schedules. Tell customers early about any risk to their parts. Source: Manufacturing Process Overview §7 and FirstGear Company Overview §4.

### Q: Which quality and safety certifications can I mention?
The customer-quality system works to IATF 16949 plus customer CSRs; the company reports ISO 14001, ISO 50001 and ISO 45001 certification and a zero lost-time injury frequency rate in FY 2025-26. Source: Customer Quality Governance Manual §1 and FirstGear Company Overview §11.
