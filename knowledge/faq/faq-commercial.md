# KAM FAQ — RFQs, Costing, Pricing and Approvals

Short answers to the commercial questions KAMs ask most. The governing documents are the RFQ to Quotation SOP, the Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide and the Commercial Approval Authority Matrix; if this FAQ and a governing document ever differ, the governing document wins.

### Q: A customer has sent an RFQ. What do I do first?
Log it in the CRM within one working day and get an RFQ number, then acknowledge receipt to the customer within two working days, confirm the submission deadline and list any missing inputs. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.1.

### Q: The RFQ is missing volumes or the SOP date. Can I start costing?
No. An RFQ is not costed until the scope is clear. Send the customer a written clarification list for missing drawings, volumes, SOP date, packaging and logistics terms. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.1.

### Q: What happens after intake and before costing?
A cross-functional feasibility review led by the KAM: Engineering confirms technical feasibility and DFM risks, the Plant confirms capacity and cycle time, SCM confirms material and lead times. It ends in a documented go / no-go decision, normally within five working days. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.2.

### Q: Feasibility came back "conditional". What now?
Explain the conditions or the no-go to the customer before any price is discussed. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.2.

### Q: Which inputs go into a quotation?
Only documented inputs: Engineering (design assumptions, DFM changes), Plant (cycle time, OEE, capacity), SCM (material specification, price basis, logistics), Quality (special characteristics, validation and PPAP effort) and Finance (overhead rates, margin targets). Record the source and date of each input in the RFQ file. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.3.

### Q: What is the quotation workflow?
Cost model → pricing proposal → internal approval → customer submission. The cost model separates piece price, tooling and development cost. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.4.

### Q: How long is a quotation valid?
90 days unless otherwise approved, and it must state the raw-material index basis used. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.4.

### Q: Who approves a quotation?
The KAM prepares but never approves. The Head of Key Accounts approves at or above the margin floor; the CFO approves below the floor or a lifetime contract value above ₹50 crore; the Managing Director also approves strategic below-floor quotations. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.5 and Approval Authority Matrix §2.

### Q: The buyer wants a ballpark price by Friday. Can I give one?
No. No quotation may be submitted and no verbal or indicative price given before approval is recorded — and a KAM in onboarding has no pricing authority at all. Offer a clear timeline for an approved quotation instead. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §3.5 and Approval Authority Matrix §1.

### Q: How is a piece price built?
Material cost (typically 55–65% of the price), conversion cost (machine and labour time from the Plant's cycle time), overheads, logistics and packaging, SG&A and margin. Every element must trace to a documented input. Source: Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide §1.

### Q: What are the margin target and margin floor?
Target contribution margin 22%; margin floor 15%. Below the floor a quotation needs CFO approval. Protect margin by challenging cost assumptions first. Source: Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide §3.

### Q: How is tooling cost recovered?
Either as a one-time lump sum from the customer or amortised into the piece price over an agreed volume. Amortisation carries volume risk; amortisation beyond three years needs CFO approval. Source: Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide §2.

### Q: How do raw-material index adjustments work?
Steel, aluminium and copper indices are reviewed quarterly with a three-month lag, both up and down. Every quotation states its index basis. Source: Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide §4.

### Q: Why do index clauses matter so much for our products?
Material cost was 47% of the company's total income in FY 2025-26 — special steels and castings for gears and differentials, copper, aluminium and magnets for motors — so index clauses protect margin. Source: Manufacturing Process Overview §7.

### Q: What is an annual productivity price-down?
A price reduction in long-term agreements, typically 1–3% a year, negotiated against productivity plans. Source: Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide §4.

### Q: The customer demands an extra price-down in a meeting. What do I say?
Listen and capture the ask in writing, do not concede in the room, analyse with Finance using the cost breakdown and index data, prepare a give-get position, get approval at the right level and reply by an agreed date. Source: Customer Escalation Playbook §4.

### Q: Who can approve a price adjustment?
The Head of Key Accounts approves adjustments within ±2% of the last approved price; anything beyond ±2% needs the CFO. Source: Approval Authority Matrix §2.

### Q: A customer wants longer payment terms. Can I agree?
No. Standard terms are 60 days and any change needs CFO approval regardless of value. Finance costs each additional 15 days before you negotiate. Source: Approval Authority Matrix §3 and Costing Guide §5.

### Q: How are engineering-change price impacts handled?
Through the RFQ process: customer-initiated changes after design freeze are quoted for price and timing before implementation. Source: Programme Governance §3 and Costing Guide §4.

### Q: Can I raise a commercial claim, for example for premium freight or volume shortfall?
Yes, with documented evidence. Agree the cost owner of premium freight before it is used. Source: Costing Guide §4 and Escalation Playbook §2.

### Q: What goes into a negotiation brief?
Objective, walk-away position, the customer's likely position, give-gets, evidence (index data, cost breakdown) and the approvals already obtained. Source: Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide §6.

### Q: The customer asks for more than my approved position. What now?
Never concede beyond the approved position in the room; take the new ask back for approval. Source: Costing and Commercial Mechanics Guide §6.

### Q: Is an approval by email or phone enough?
Only if it is recorded with the approver, date, value and basis. Undocumented approvals are invalid. Source: Approval Authority Matrix §6.

### Q: What are the most common RFQ failure modes?
Quoting before feasibility, indicating verbal prices, using undocumented inputs, missing the raw-material index basis and submitting without recorded approval. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §5.

### Q: When do I get pricing authority as a new KAM?
Only after the Day-21 scenario test (Gate 2), a Green Day-15 result and a recorded Reporting Boss approval for guided pricing exposure — and then only to prepare positions decided jointly with the Reporting Boss. Source: Approval Authority Matrix §1 and Readiness Assessment Guide §6.

### Q: What does the RFQ file contain?
The intake log, feasibility record, inputs, cost model, approval record and the submitted quotation. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP §4.

### Q: A competitor's sales manager asks about our pricing at an industry event. What do I do?
Do not discuss prices, margins, capacity or bidding intentions with competitors. Leave the conversation and report it to Legal. Source: Code of Conduct §3.
