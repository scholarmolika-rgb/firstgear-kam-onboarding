# KAM FAQ — Account Ownership, Governance and Conduct

How a KAM runs an account day to day. The governing documents are the KAM Charter, Organisational Navigation and Knowledge Sources, the Code of Conduct and the Customer 360 records.

### Q: What does the KAM own, and what do functions own?
The KAM owns the customer relationship and communication, commercial negotiation within approved authority, the account plan and coordination of cross-functional actions. Engineering owns technical solutions, Quality owns containment and 8D, SCM and the Plant own delivery, Finance owns cost models and credit. The KAM makes sure the work happens — it does not do the functions' work. Source: KAM Charter §2.

### Q: What is the governance calendar for an account?
A weekly internal account review led by the KAM (quality, delivery, commercial and programme topics), a monthly customer operations review with purchasing and quality, and a quarterly business review (QBR) with customer leadership, prepared with the Reporting Boss. Source: KAM Charter §3.

### Q: Which KPIs am I measured on?
Revenue versus plan, on-time-in-full delivery (OTIF), customer PPM, RFQ hit rate, receivables (DSO) and customer satisfaction. Source: KAM Charter §4.

### Q: Who owns which decision inside the company?
Engineering — design, DFM, engineering change notices. NPD — launches and APQP. Quality — customer quality, containment, 8D, PPAP. SCM — planning, schedules, supplier risk, premium freight (Head of SCM). Plant — production, capacity, OEE, OTIF. Finance — cost models, overhead rates, margin targets, credit and payment terms. Source: Organisational Navigation §1.

### Q: How do I get something done across functions?
Start with the owner of the decision, not the most senior person; bring facts and a clear ask; use the weekly internal account review for cross-functional issues. Source: Organisational Navigation §2.

### Q: Where do I find reliable information?
Only in approved, current documents in the Compass knowledge base and in approved Customer 360 records. If a source is unapproved or out of date, ask the document owner. Source: Organisational Navigation §3.

### Q: What goes into a Customer 360?
Organisation, strategic context, supplied parts and applications, programmes and volumes, pipeline, pricing and commercial history, open commitments and lessons learned. Source: Customer 360 — Northwind Motors.

### Q: How do I build a stakeholder map?
Map at least purchasing, engineering, quality and SCM/plant contacts on the customer side, with their influence and relationship status, and keep it in the Customer 360. Source: Customer 360 — Northwind Motors §1.

### Q: How do I prepare a QBR?
With the Reporting Boss: cover the KPIs (OTIF, PPM, revenue versus plan), open commitments and 8D status, programme milestones, pipeline, and any commercial topics — using only approved figures. Source: KAM Charter §3–4.

### Q: What can I tell a customer about our capacity or future plans?
Only approved, published information, and dates confirmed in writing by SCM and the Plant. Never share one customer's drawings, prices or programmes with another. Source: Approval Authority Matrix §5 and Code of Conduct §4.

### Q: A buyer offers me an expensive gift. Can I accept it?
Gifts above a nominal ₹5,000 must be declared to Compliance and are normally declined; cash or cash equivalents are never acceptable. Source: Code of Conduct §1.

### Q: Can I offer hospitality to a customer to help win a nomination?
No. No payment, favour or benefit may be offered to a customer employee to win or keep business. Source: Code of Conduct §2.

### Q: How do I raise an ethics concern?
With your manager, HR or the confidential ethics line, without fear of retaliation. Source: Code of Conduct §5.

### Q: What is confidential?
Customer drawings, prices and programme information. Share them only with colleagues who need them, never with other customers. Source: Code of Conduct §4.

### Q: The customer is consolidating suppliers. How should I respond?
Understand their scorecard (for Northwind: quality PPM, OTIF, cost competitiveness and EV readiness), show evidence on each, and raise it at the internal account review so functions can act. Source: Customer 360 — Northwind Motors §2.

### Q: How do I talk about EV readiness with a customer?
Use published facts: EV differential assemblies with final-drive gear, traction motors and controllers, 67 EV programmes across 35 customers, and BEV at 35% of automotive revenue in FY 2025-26. Source: Product Portfolio Guide §6 and FirstGear Company Overview §7.

### Q: What does "learn first, then earn access" mean for me?
A new or reassigned KAM gains customer and pricing exposure only through readiness gates and Reporting Boss approval — never automatically with time. Source: KAM Charter §6.

### Q: What lessons from past accounts should I remember?
Customers value early warning — late communication damages trust more than the problem itself. Always confirm dates with SCM in writing before sharing them. Source: Customer 360 — Northwind Motors §8.
