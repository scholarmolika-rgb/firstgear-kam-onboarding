# KAM FAQ — 30-Day Onboarding Journey and the Compass App

How the KAM 30-Day Readiness Journey works. The governing documents are the Readiness Assessment Guide and the KAM Charter.

### Q: What is the structure of the 30-day journey?
Phase 1 (Days 1–15) — learn about the company across Governance, People, Process and Product, with no pricing and no customer exposure. Phase 2 (Days 16–30) — practise, then own: shadow reviews (Days 16–17), own low-risk queries (18–19), lead an internal review (20), scenario test (21), guided pricing (22–25), own the account (26–29) and the readiness panel sign-off (30). Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §6.

### Q: What are the three gates?
Gate 1 (Day 15) — Phase-1 learning done, Day-10 check passed, Customer 360 and account brief reviewed by the Mentor, and readiness score of 80% or more. Gate 2 (Day 21) — scenario test certified by the Mentor. Gate 3 (Day 30) — readiness panel sign-off by the Reporting Boss. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §6.

### Q: Do I become ready automatically after 30 days?
No. Customer access is earned by passing gates, not by days passing. Only the Reporting Boss's Day-30 decision makes a KAM ready. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §5 and KAM Charter §6.

### Q: Can I work ahead of the calendar?
Yes, inside an open phase. The calendar only decides what is due or overdue; gates decide what is unlocked. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §6.

### Q: What is the Day-10 check and what happens if I fail it?
A short interim check on Days 1–10 content with a pass mark set by HR (default 70%). It is a checkpoint, not a gate: you can retake it, and the Day-15 assessment opens only once it is passed. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §1.

### Q: How is the Day-15 assessment scored?
Questions cover Governance, People, Process and Product; each pillar score is the share of marks earned, and the overall score is weighted Governance 25%, People 20%, Process 30%, Product 25% (HR can change the weights). Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §2.

### Q: What do Green, Amber and Red mean?
Green (80%+) — Gate 1 can pass and Phase 2 opens. Amber (60–79%) — a targeted 3–5 day refresh on the weakest pillars, supervised shadowing allowed, scenario test and pricing blocked until a re-check reaches 80%. Red (below 60%) — Phase 2 paused and a remediation plan agreed, with a possible extension. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §3.

### Q: What is the Day-21 scenario test?
Four scenarios — RFQ response, price challenge, delivery risk and quality escalation — scored against a rubric and compared with your Day-15 baseline. The average must reach the pass mark (default 75%) and the Mentor must certify; the Mentor cannot certify below the threshold. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §4 and §6.

### Q: How do I score well on the scenarios?
Follow the governing procedures: log and acknowledge, clarify missing inputs, run feasibility before price, never give a ballpark price, route approvals correctly, contain first in quality issues, confirm dates with SCM and the Plant before sharing them, and tell the customer early. Red flags such as quoting a price without approval carry fixed penalties. Source: RFQ to Quotation SOP and Customer Escalation Playbook.

### Q: When can I take part in pricing discussions?
From Day 22, only after Gate 2, a Green Day-15 result and a recorded Reporting Boss approval for guided pricing exposure. Positions are prepared by you and decided jointly with the Reporting Boss. Source: Approval Authority Matrix §1.

### Q: When can I own the account?
From Day 26, after Gate 2 and a Reporting Boss approval for guided customer ownership, with daily and then alternate-day review. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §6.

### Q: Who is on the Day-30 readiness panel?
The Reporting Boss, the Mentor and HR. The Mentor and HR give their inputs and the Reporting Boss records the final decision: READY, EXTENDED or NOT READY. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §5.

### Q: What happens if my onboarding is extended?
You follow the development actions agreed by the Reporting Boss for the extension period, and the panel decision is taken again at the end. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §5.

### Q: Why can't I tick some tasks myself?
Some tasks are owned by your Mentor, Reporting Boss or HR, and some complete automatically when you finish the linked action (an assessment, a scenario or the account brief). Tasks marked "needs reviewer approval" are submitted for review when you tick them. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §6.

### Q: What must my account brief contain before I can submit it?
The required sections (customer organisation, strategic context, supplied parts, programmes, pricing history, open commitments, lessons learned) and a stakeholder map with at least four stakeholders — purchasing, engineering, quality and SCM/plant. Source: Customer 360 — Northwind Motors.

### Q: What can the Ask FirstGear assistant do?
Answer questions from approved documents with the source shown, explain your progress, gates and next steps from your recorded data, and pass a question to your Mentor or Reporting Boss. It never approves anything, quotes prices or decides readiness. Source: Organisational Navigation §3.

### Q: The assistant says it has insufficient evidence. Why?
It answers only from approved, current documents. If nothing relevant is found it says so rather than guessing — ask your Mentor or the document owner. Source: Organisational Navigation §3.

### Q: Who do I go to for help during onboarding?
Your Mentor for coaching and reviews, your Reporting Boss for expectations and approvals, and HR for programme questions. Source: KAM Charter §6.

### Q: How do I move between pages in the Compass app?
Use the left menu, or the Previous / Next buttons at the bottom of each page, which follow the menu order. Journey day pages have Day N−1 / Day N+1 buttons. Source: Readiness Assessment Guide §6.
